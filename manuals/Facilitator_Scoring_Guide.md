## 1. Script & Lesson Plan Adherence (Module 3, Lesson 3.2)
| DO | DON'T |
| :--- | :--- |
| Follow the lesson plan sequence and ask the scripted questions in the Facilitator Guide. | Skip scripted questions, improvise your own curriculum, or turn CBT class into general life-advice therapy. |
| Keep **Homework Review to 15–18 minutes max**; verify 100% completion (front and back, every question answered). | Spend 30+ minutes re-teaching yesterday's lesson during Homework Review or award credit for partially blank worksheets. |
| **Protect the Main Activity / Behavioral Practice above all else** by front-loading time limits and milestones. | Let early discussion run long and rush or skip the main worksheet or role-play practice at the end of class. |
| Watch for literacy barriers (high verbal skill but blank/minimal writing) and support discreetly. | Call out or embarrass a participant who struggles with reading or writing in front of the group. |

## 2. Communication & SYNAPSE Core Practices (Module 2 & SYNAPSE)
| DO | DON'T |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ask open-ended questions (*"What thought came right before that action?"*) and wait for participants to think. | Ask closed yes/no questions (*"Did that make you mad?"*) or immediately jump in to give the "right" answer. |
| **Pivot to the Group** after a participant shares (*"Who else sees how that thought leads to that action?"*). | Comment after every single participant speaks or hold 1-on-1 conversations while the rest of the room watches. |
| Maintain **Neutrality** when participants share thoughts, beliefs, or past behavior—guide back to the lesson objective. | Show approval/co-sign antisocial stories OR show disapproval, shock, or moral judgment. |
| Use **Situated Empathy & Yielding** (paraphrase content, reflect emotion early, confirm *"Is that close?"*). | Debate a participant, tell them how they *should* feel, or push for deep insight when they are flooded. |

## 3. Pro-Social Modeling: Classroom Facilitation (Module 1, Lesson 1.4)
| DO | DON'T |
| :--- | :--- |
| Model self-control, preparedness, punctuality, and respect for every rule you ask participants to follow. | Break classroom rules, look distracted, talk down to participants, or complain about the program/workplace. |
| Separate the **person from the behavior** (*"Talking while someone else is sharing disrupts the group"*). | Label or attack character (*"You're being disrespectful"* / *"You're not taking this seriously"*). |
| Require participants to trace external triggers back to their **internal thinking** ($S \rightarrow T \rightarrow A \rightarrow C$). | Allow external blame (*"My PO/boss made me do it"*) to stand unchallenged without isolating the thought. |

## 4. Pro-Social Modeling: Role-Play & Behavioral Practice (Module 3, Lesson 3.3)
| DO | DON'T |
| :--- | :--- |
| Pre-approve realistic role-play situations tied to the participant's actual risk patterns during prep time. | Allow participants to pick trivial/fake scenarios on the spot or switch situations last-minute without checking in. |
| **Set the Stage out loud before saying "Action"** (Main Actor, Co-Actor role, situation, format, and steps to watch). | Say *"Who wants to go first? Action!"* without confirming roles, setting, and format for the room. |
| Model the skill first or send the strongest pair up first; confirm the ending action step before starting. | Let weaker pairs go first without a model or start a scene without knowing where it ends. |
| Say **"Cut"** as soon as the final skill step is reached (or cut mid-scene immediately if off-rails/criminal behavior). | Let a role play drag on past the skill ending or let a derailed Co-Actor ruin the Main Actor's practice. |
| **Debrief the Main Actor first**; proactively reinforce positive internal consequences. | Critique the Main Actor before asking how they felt, or speculate/assume negative guilt they didn't express. |

## 5. Managing Challenging Behavior & Resistance (Module 4)
| DO | DON'T |
| :--- | :--- |
| Address disruptions early, calmly, and matter-of-factly using established classroom expectations. | Ignore minor disruptions until you lose your cool, or engage in public power struggles. |
| Roll with resistance by offering choices, connecting to the participant's own goals, and keeping a calm tone. | Argue, try to force compliance in front of the group, or take resistance personally. |

## 1. Strict Separation: Classroom Facilitation Modeling vs. Role-Play Modeling
Evaluations MUST separate modeling into two distinct categories so facilitators receive accurate feedback on each:

### Category A: Classroom Facilitation Modeling (General Instruction & Discussion)
How the facilitator carries themselves and models pro-social, non-criminogenic behavior throughout the class outside of role-play scenes:
- **Behavioral Modeling (Visible Standards):**
  - Starting on time, having materials/board ready, following all classroom rules, and holding consistent expectations.
- **Skill-Based Modeling (Real-Time Responses):**
  - Staying calm and regulated when challenged or interrupted.
  - **Separating the Person from the Behavior (Module 1, p. 38):** Addressing observable actions (*"When side conversations happen while someone is sharing, it disrupts the group"*) rather than labeling the person (*"You're being disrespectful"*).
  - **Modeling Neutrality (Lesson 1.5 Add-On):** Never co-signing criminal thinking, street code, or external blame, AND never judging or lecturing. Keeping discussion anchored to $S \rightarrow T \rightarrow A \rightarrow C$.
  - **Reframing Anti-Social/Blaming Statements:** Prompting participants to identify their own internal thought rather than arguing with them.

### Category B: Role-Play & Behavioral Practice Modeling (Lesson 3.3)
How the facilitator sets up, models, directs, and debriefs structured behavioral practice (role plays):
1. **Situation Approval (Before Role Play Begins):**
   - Must approve a realistic setback tied to the participant's actual risk patterns during prep time. Once approved, the situation is locked.
2. **Setting the Stage Out Loud (Before Saying "Action"):**
   - The facilitator MUST confirm four things out loud to the entire room before saying *"Action"*:
     1. **Main Actor** (must play themselves).
     2. **Co-Actor's Role** (who they are playing in the Main Actor's life).
     3. **The Situation** (what is happening).
     4. **Scene Format & Starting Point** (phone call or in-person, where they are, and what skill steps the audience is watching for).
   - When actors flip roles, the facilitator must reset the stage out loud before saying *"Action"* again.
3. **Modeling First / Sequencing Pairs:**
   - The facilitator models the skill first (or sends the strongest pair up first) so the group sees a clear pro-social demonstration before weaker pairs practice.
4. **Begin with the End in Mind & Saying "Cut":**
   - Confirm the final action step before starting.
   - Say **"Cut"** immediately when the Main Actor completes the final skill step.
   - **Mid-Scene Cut (When Necessary):** Cut immediately if a Co-Actor derails the scene or anyone acts out criminal/violent behavior; do a brief huddle to regroup and restart with *"Action"*.
5. **Debriefing Order & Internal/External Consequences:**
   - **Always debrief the Main Actor first** (*"How did that feel? Which steps did you hit?"*) before asking the Co-Actor or audience.
   - **Proactively reinforce positive internal consequences** (how the participant felt about themselves for handling it well).
   - **Never assume or probe for unspoken negative internal consequences (guilt)** if the participant didn't bring them up themselves.

## 2. Facilitator Talk-Time & Give-and-Take ("Holding the Rope" — Lesson 1.5)
- **Target Balance:** CBT is collaborative, not a lecture. Participants learn through cognitive work and behavioral practice, not listening to the facilitator talk.
- **Deterministic Talk-Time Thresholds:**
  - **Healthy Give-and-Take (35%–55% Facilitator Words):** Facilitator asks scripted open questions, lets discussion grow (*"Magic Moments"* where participants respond to each other), and pulls the rope back only when discussion drifts.
  - **Caution Zone (56%–65% Facilitator Words):** Facilitator is doing too much explaining or responding to every single participant comment instead of pivoting to the group.
  - **Talking Too Much / "Holding the Rope Too Tight" (> 65% Facilitator Words or Uninterrupted Monologues > 90 seconds):**
    - Symptom 1: Facilitator answers their own questions or lectures for minutes at a time.
    - Symptom 2: One-on-one ping-pong (`Facilitator -> Participant A -> Facilitator -> Participant B`) with zero participant-to-participant interaction.
    - Symptom 3: Over-explaining during Homework Review (exceeding the **15–18 minute maximum** by re-teaching the previous lesson instead of checking 100% front-and-back completion and reinforcing key concepts).
  - **"The Wilderness" (Unchecked Drift):** Participant talk-time is high, but discussion has drifted into unrelated war stories or arguments without the facilitator pulling the rope back to the lesson objective.

## 1. The SYNAPSE Framework in Facilitation & Coaching
- **GPCSL Foundation:** General Personality and Cognitive Social Learning — addressing cognitive patterns, emotional regulation (*Baggage Claim*: reading the emotional license plate before acting), and environmental/social contingencies together.
- **S — Situate (Situated Empathy):**
  - Calm amygdala alarm states by grounding listening in the participant's real environment and pressures.
  - **Do:** Let participants articulate their lived context before jumping to problem-solving; ask permission before giving advice (*"Would you like an option that's worked for others here?"*).
  - **Don't:** Rush into clinical clichés or give unsolicited advice before understanding the situation.
- **Y — Yield (Reflective Listening Loop):**
  - Move interaction from stress/defensiveness into executive functioning clarity.
  - **Paraphrase:** Translate content into clear language without parroting word-for-word.
  - **Reflect Feeling Early:** Name the underlying emotion before the participant jumps from event to self-judgment or blame.
  - **Confirm & Adjust Loop:** Check accuracy (*"Is that close?"*) and adjust immediately when the participant refines it.
- **N — Navigate (Balancing Dissonance / The Scale):**
  - Surface discrepancy between stated goals/values and recent actions without shaming, debating, or arguing the other side out of existence.
  - **Do:** Use double-sided reflections (*"On one hand you want [Goal], and on the other hand [Behavior] happened this week. How do you see those fitting together?"*).
  - **Don't:** Say *"You're contradicting yourself"* or lecture on why their thinking is wrong.
- **A — Activate (NAME Motivation Anchors):**
  - Move from hollow compliance (*"I know I should"*) to genuine ownership by anchoring change in **N**ecessity, **A**ptitude (self-efficacy), **M**otive (what sacrifice protects), and **E**njoyment.
- **P — Pictures (Survival Picture -> Cost Picture -> New Picture):**
  - Acknowledge how an old behavior once served a survival function, surface its present **Cost Picture** (what it costs them now), and connect plain-language strengths to a concrete **New Picture**.
- **S — Splash (Splashmapping):**
  - Map immediate 1–2 day **Good Ripples** and **Bad Ripples** of a choice; keep action steps low in count and realistic for daily stress; set a specific review window and adjustment trigger.
- **E — Engineering (Neuro-Resilience & TLCs):**
  - Support behavioral plasticity through practical routines (sleep, stress regulation, daily structure); when a plan stalls, **redesign rather than punish**.

## 2. Core Classroom Communication Moves (Trainer Workbook Module 2)
1. **Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions (Lesson 2.2):**
   - Use open questions (*"What..."*, *"How..."*, *"Walk us through..."*) to prompt cognitive work. Avoid leading or yes/no questions that let participants stay passive.
2. **Pivot to the Group (Lesson 2.1):**
   - After one participant answers, redirect the conversation to the rest of the room (*"Who else has been in a spot like that?"*, *"How does that connect to what was shared earlier?"*) rather than turning class into a 1-on-1 dialogue between facilitator and one participant.
3. **Reflective Listening & Summarizing (Lesson 2.3):**
   - Reflect core meaning and tie participant comments directly back to the lesson objective.
4. **Specific Praise & Reinforcement (Lesson 2.4):**
   - Reinforce specific pro-social thinking and skill steps (*"Notice how you paused at the Thinking step before acting"*), not generic praise (*"Good job"*).
5. **Modeling Neutrality (Lesson 1.5 Add-On):**
   - When participants share past criminal behavior, beliefs, or triggers, respond without shock, judgment, approval, or co-signing. Guide the focus back to the cognitive/behavioral skill.
