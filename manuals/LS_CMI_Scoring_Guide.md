Chapter 2
The LS/CMI system is administered and scored in paper-
and-pencil format using the LS/CMI Interview Guide,
Offender History Form, QuikScore Form (Sections 1 to
8), Case Management Protocol Form (Sections 9 to 11),
and ColorPlot™ Profile Form. The assessor should note
that the LS/CMI QuickScore Form includes all of the nec-
essary elements for scoring the instrument. There are no
conventional scoring templates for Section 1 (the only
scored section of the assessment), as the QuikScore Form
contains special aids that make scoring of the test very
rapid, and simultaneously minimizes the potential for
errors. Responses transfer to an underlying page that suc-
cinctly summarizes the key information. Scores can con-
veniently be plotted on the ColorPlot Profile Form, which
allows for a quick comparison to a large normative base
and provides criteria for making decisions regarding insti-
tutional classification or community supervision level/pro-
gram placement.
To complete the QuikScore Form, the administrator will
need to collect information available from file records, and
will need to conduct an interview with the offender to
obtain responses to the LS/CMI questions. Although some
variation in the standard administration procedure is per-
mitted, the Interview Guide provides a protocol that has
been found to be effective in obtaining the information
required to complete the LS/CMI QuikScore form.
Age Range
The LS/CMI system is appropriate for use with male and
female offenders who are 16 years of age or older. There is
no upper age limit. The Youth Level of Service/Case Man-
agement Inventory (YLS/CMIT; Hoge & Andrews, 2002)
should be used with offenders under the age of 16, and in
some cases with young offenders over age 16.
Offender History Form
The Offender History Form records assessment rationale
and offender history information for a given LS/CMI
assessment. This form is composed of two sections:
Reason for Assessment and General Criminal History
and Offence Information.
Sample Offender History Forms illustrating how to
complete these sections are included with the case studies

A. Reason for Assessment
This section of the Offender History Form allows the
LS/CMI user to classify the reason for the offender's
assessment or re-assessment. Choose the offender's sta-

checking the "Other" box include immigration hearings,
pre-charge diversions, alternative measures, or referral to
social or mental health services. Specify the reason in the
space provided. For institutional offenders, some examples
of "Other" reasons for assessment may include bail hear-

provided.
If the offender is being reassessed and this is a routine
reassessment, check the appropriate box under Section
A.4. If it is not a routine reassessment, check the "Other"
box under Section A.4 and specify the reason in the space
provided.
Whenever the LS/CMI system makes up part of a psycho-

Strength Notation
Each of the eight General Risk/Need subcomponents
requires that the assessor judge if a given area is consid-
ered as one of strength. This innovation is a response to
the opinions of many correctional professionals. Note that
it is the subcomponents and not individual items that are
to be considered as a strength. Many professionals have
called for the opportunity to record exceptionally posi-
tive circumstances which could be used advantageously
in formulating case plans. This innovation is also a
response to research evidence that some conditions, by
virtue of their presence, may serve as protective factors
and actively reduce the chances of antisocial conduct.
Marking Strength for any of the General Risk/Need sub-
components will reflect a judgement of the following type.
• The assessment of a particular subtotal area indicates
not only that it represents a very low or low risk
domain, but also that the circumstances or level of
functioning are so positive that they may reduce the
influence of other risk factors that are present.
Example. Companions may be assessed as a risk factor,
but personal attitudes are so anticriminal that the effect
of companions is minimized.
• Assessment within a particular subtotal area indicates
a circumstance or level of functioning that is incom-
patible with a reasonable expectation of antisocial
conduct.
Example. Antisocial friends may be scored present,
but the degree of influence of antisocial friends is far
outweighed by the influence of prosocial associates.
That is, although the offender may have friends that
are both antisocial and prosocial, the attachment and
respect given to prosocial associates is so strong that
antisocial influences are rendered less important.

need, and responsivity to be most effective. The case

Correctional agencies often refer to this person as a clas-

mental health facility). Therefore, some kind of legal deter-
mination is required. This may include a breach of condi-
tion (bail, probation, community condition), a suspension
or revocation of the release conditions (parole), new

11.2 Discharge Summary Narrative. The discharge sum-
mary is often neglected in the case management process.
Yet, it serves three important functions. First, it affords an
opportunity for the case manager and associated team to
reflect specifically on the progress or lack thereof for each
offender. For the LS/CMI Discharge Summary, the pro-
cess should focus on the extent to which the offender's
needs have been addressed and the offender's risk has
been affected. Secondly, it forms a basis for future contact
with the offender, either in the event of failure or transfer
to another organization. Finally, by following a semi-
standardized format, it lends itself to a more general
review or evaluation of offender services by the agency.
In consultation with other staff and possibly the offender,
the case manager should report on the status of the
offender at discharge/completion. This includes the degree
to which criminogenic and noncriminogenic needs were
addressed and satisfied, any problems that may have
occurred in the attempt to deliver programs and services,
the extent to which responsivity concerns were accom-
modated, and the degree to which offender risk may have
been affected by these efforts. In the event of program
failure, some attention should be given to areas that may
have been overlooked and new needs that may have
become evident.
Authorization and Sign-off
The case manager's signature in this section indicates
either that the case is being closed or that the file will be
forwarded to another agency, jurisdiction, or branch of
the criminal justice system. As was the case in Section 8,
the Discharge Summary affords an opportunity for agen-
cies that require administrative sign-off by a supervisor or
manager to do so in the space provided (authorization).
