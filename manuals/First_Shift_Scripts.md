# Turn90 First Shift Program — Extracted & Rewritten Scripts

> **Source:** Extracted from *Turn90 Program Manager Guide 3.0* and rewritten to align with the **First Shift Program** structure and consequences (*Turn90 Playbook*).

---

## Key Program & Consequence Changes (3.0 Guide → First Shift)

| Area | Old 3.0 Guide (16-Week Model) | First Shift Program (8-Week Model) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Program Length & Structure** | 16 weeks (10 weeks half-day CBT + half-day print shop, 6 weeks full-time print shop). | **8-week rapid employment program** (CBT classes, Skillcat, Briefcase, 4-Week Job Placement Readiness Gates, weekly Case Management). **Transitional Jobs (TJ)** is a separate in-house work track if someone needs more time and a spot is open. |
| **Leveling / Points** | Leveling up via 45 points back-to-back weeks; moving back a level or forfeiting weekly points; 2-week program extensions. | No 2-week program extensions. Progress is tracked via attendance, CBT/Skillcat/Briefcase participation, and **4-Week Readiness Gates**. |
| **Unplanned Absences** | Lose points/pay; delayed level-up. | **2 or more unplanned absences** = **Stability Issue** → moved out of First Shift back into **Reentry Navigation** (override requires Program Director or Executive Director). |
| **No Call / No Show (NCNS)** | 1st NCNS = suspended enrollment + PM meeting; multiple = termination / restart later. | **ANY NCNS** = **Stability Issue** → moved out of First Shift back into **Reentry Navigation** (or exited/terminated; override requires Program Director or Executive Director). |
| **Smelling like MJ / Alcohol** | MJ smell + negative test = swap shirt; MJ smell + positive test = sent home, move back a level, forfeit points; Alcohol smell = sent home, delayed level-up. | **Smelling like marijuana or alcohol** = **Stability Issue** → moved out of First Shift back into **Reentry Navigation**; on-site impairment = immediate removal leading to suspension or exit. |
| **Drug Testing** | MJ at enrollment = classes only, no stipend until negative; 30+ days MJ = case-by-case; 3rd positive = termination. | **THC only** (no on-site impairment/impact) may remain active. **Hard substances** (meth, cocaine, opiates) = termination (rare isolated exception under close supervision). **Refusal / leaving before testing / tampering** = immediate termination/exit. |
| **Ineligibility / Waitlist** | Referred out or placed on passive waitlist. | Routed to **Reentry Navigation** (in-person stabilization & Briefcase prep; gate to First Shift) or **Resource Coordination** (phone triage/referrals). Max 6 enrolled per center per location. |

## 1. Enrollment & Eligibility Scripts

### 1.1 Quick Eligibility Script
*(Original: Guide p. 22)*

> “Thanks for calling, I just need to get some information from you if that’s alright. *(Get eligibility information and set appointment/consultation if eligible.)* Do you have any questions about what we do here? We work with men who have been recently released from prison or jail. Our **First Shift** program is an 8-week rapid employment program where you attend classes, build your career Briefcase and certifications, and work with a case manager to get ready for job placement. While you’re in First Shift, you earn a stipend based on attendance and participation. If you need extra time to get stable—like housing, IDs, or transportation—we can start you in **Reentry Navigation** first until you’re ready for First Shift. At the end of the 8 weeks, we connect you with an employer willing to give you an opportunity based on your performance here. Do you have any questions about any of that?”

---

### 1.2 Responding to Ineligibility Scripts
*(Original: Guide p. 23)*

#### Pending Charges
> “It looks like you have some more serious charges still pending. That’s going to be a barrier for **First Shift** right now because it’s an 8-week rapid employment program and employers need to know you’re able to stay on the job without court or incarceration interrupting your work. The worst thing that could happen is that you put in the work here and get placed, only to end up going back to prison. We need reassurance from your PD or probation officer that the outcome in court won’t block you from working. Can you ask your PD or probation officer to send me [or the program manager] an email about you wanting to enroll? While we sort that out, we can connect you with **Resource Coordination / Reentry Navigation** to help you with stabilization.”

#### Outside of Age Range
> “I’m glad you called, and it sounds like you’re really trying to make some changes in your life. That’s great. Unfortunately, our First Shift program only works with men between the ages of 25 and 50, and you fall outside of that age range. We have resources focused on that specific group, so we can’t enroll you in First Shift—but through **Resource Coordination**, I can give you referrals for job training or employment if you’re interested.”

#### Lack of Stable Housing
> “It sounds like you have some housing issues that you’re trying to work out. For **First Shift**, you have to be stable enough to complete the full 8-week rapid employment program—including having a consistent place to sleep at night so you aren’t worried about where you’re staying while trying to get to class and start a job. Because housing instability would put you at risk of falling out of First Shift, let’s start you in **Reentry Navigation** [or **Resource Coordination**] first so we can work on housing stabilization and transitional housing or shelter options. Once your housing is stable, we can look at moving you into First Shift.”

#### Released More Than 6–12 Months Ago
> “We focus First Shift on men who have been recently released from jail or prison. We keep our cohorts small—only six spots per center—so we hold those spots for men who need immediate help making the transition from prison. I’m sorry about that. It sounds like employment is your biggest barrier right now. Through **Resource Coordination**, would you be interested in information on a few employers we know hire people with felony records?”

## 2. Assessment Scripts

### 2.1 Opening Script
*(Original: Guide p. 28)*

> “Hello, my name is ____________ and I am the program manager here. Our goal is to help you get into stable employment while also helping you identify thinking patterns that may have held you back in the past. We’re here to support you in reaching the goals you want to reach.
>
> **First Shift** is an 8-week rapid employment program. Over these 8 weeks, we work with you on your **Briefcase**—things like your ID, resume, Skillcat training track, and certifications—while meeting weekly for case management and attending CBT classes. Because it’s a fast-paced 8-week program, you have to stay stable and show up consistently. If major stability issues come up—like missing two or more days, having a No Call/No Show, or substance or housing issues—you’ll step back into **Reentry Navigation** so we can help you get stabilized before trying First Shift again.
>
> We have relationships with employers who offer benefits, competitive pay, and career paths. They’re willing to hire you after you complete First Shift because they know you showed up on time, stayed drug-free, worked well with others, followed directions, and put in the work on your thinking patterns and readiness gates. The core of what we do in class is based on ‘what we think determines what we do—our thoughts control our actions.’ Do you have any questions about any of that?”

### 2.3 Assessment FAQ Model Scripts
*(Original: Guide p. 53–54)*

#### More Than 15 Minutes Late Affecting Staff Schedule

> “Before we start the assessment today, I want to talk quickly about the fact that you smell like weed. First Shift is a rapid employment program with strict stability expectations. Also, employers we place you with drug test. We’ll work with you if you test positive for THC at intake as long as you aren’t impaired on-site and it doesn’t affect your participation, but you cannot arrive smelling like weed. Is that something you can commit to if you start? Does that sound okay?”

#### Dishonesty During Assessment (Verifiable Discrepancy)
> “You’re telling me that you didn’t have any arrests from 2015–2018, but when I look in the jail records, I see that you had a PWID arrest in 2016. Can you tell me about that?”

## 3. Case Management Scripts

### 3.1 Getting Buy-In on a Current Issue
*(Original: Guide p. 64)*

> “You said that you had an issue with your probation officer this week. Can you tell me more about that? *[student gives details]* I remember from your timeline that not getting along with authority has caused you some problems. In First Shift, we only have 8 weeks to get you job-ready, and any supervision or legal conflict can derail your placement. If you continue to have confrontations with authority, like your probation officer, how do you think that might affect your ability to stay in First Shift and reach your goals, like starting your own business or being a good dad? *[student answers]* Exactly. How about we practice a skill today that you can use to minimize problems with authority?”

### 3.2 Switching to Another Target
*(Original: Guide p. 64)*

> “Okay, no problem. I was curious about something. You mentioned one of the areas you struggle with is being impulsive—making decisions on the spot without thinking them through. You said being impulsive had caused you some problems at work and on probation. How do you think being impulsive might get in the way of finishing these 8 weeks and reaching your goals, like buying a house or landing a job that pays well?”

### 3.3 Values Session Script
*(Original: Guide p. 65–66)*

> “I just wanted to cover a few things as a reminder if that’s okay? Great. So my job here is to help you reach your goals. Think of me as a personal assistant—you can ask me for help in any area you want assistance in. Our goal is to help you get placed in a solid job through First Shift and help you identify thinking patterns that you want to work on. It is completely your decision to identify which patterns have caused problems. We are your team, you’re the boss. You decide what you want us to do. Cool? Cool.
>
> You remember the idea that thinking controls actions? That idea is one we will continue to call back to. I also wanted to talk about inside and outside triggers and consequences. *(Talk about inside and outside consequences and triggers. Outside triggers: ‘They don’t control us. We can’t control them.’)*
>
> Great. I was also hoping to spend some time today to talk about your goals and what you care about. How does that sound? Awesome. What are your values?”

### 3.4 Antisocial Personality Pattern (APP) Session Script
*(Original: Guide p. 66–67)*

> “[Client’s name], we are all guided by our thinking. As we go through life, we develop rules for how we interpret things, see ourselves, and react to others. With years of repetition, much of our thinking becomes automatic and inflexible, and we become much less aware of how some of our most important thinking patterns guide our everyday decisions. Those habits are sometimes called mechanisms. Of course, for all of us, our own thinking seems perfectly normal, because we have repeated many of the same thoughts in our heads for years.
>
> It is possible for people to live with heart disease and have few noticeable symptoms; to remain relatively unaware of how their current lifestyles undermine their long-term well-being; and not consider a change until they suffer a cardiac event. In the same way, committing crimes over a long period of time has been described as a lifestyle disorder that develops over time and affects judgment and behavior. People can remain relatively unaware of the lifestyle disorder but continue to suffer from the consequences.
>
> The goal is to become aware of the lifestyle factors—thinking patterns, ways of dealing with conflict and stress, people, places, things—and make decisions on which factors will help you reach your goals and which ones will not. Unfortunately, most people have thought patterns that can cause them problems, and a lot of people have developed what is called antisocial personality pattern. Part of our work together will involve creating an awareness of some of the thinking patterns that have developed for you and exploring the effect they’ve had on your life. Only you can decide if a thinking pattern is working for you or not. Today, I just wanted to cover the antisocial personality pattern—how does that sound? *(Cover APP)*
>
> Now that we have covered them, which ones do you think caused you the most problems? Maybe the top two or three. *(Ask the client to pick three and tie those actions to thinking patterns.)* Great, I really appreciate you exploring this with me.”

### 3.5 Low Motivation / Unmotivated in Case Management
*(Original: Guide p. 70)*

> “I hear you that you don’t think you have anything to work on today, but remember, you agreed to work on this case plan and your First Shift readiness gates to help yourself be successful. First Shift is only 8 weeks, and we don’t have time to coast. If you feel like you have done all the work you need to do, I completely understand—I can give you a referral through Resource Coordination or a job agency and we can move on. No hard feelings on our end. How about we call it a day and I will check back in a day or two so you can think about whether First Shift is what you want right now?”

## 4. Responding to Common Behavioral Issues — Rewritten Scripts

### 4.1 Missing Class or Work (Unplanned Absences)
*(Original: Guide p. 73 | First Shift Rule: **2 or more unplanned absences** is a Stability Issue → moved out of First Shift back into Reentry Navigation unless overridden by Program Director / Executive Director.)*

#### 1. Student calls in to say he can’t make it in because of a last-minute emergency
> “I understand you have a situation happening. I hate for you to miss today because First Shift moves fast—it’s only 8 weeks, and our policy is that **two or more unplanned absences** is a stability issue that moves you out of First Shift and back into **Reentry Navigation** until things settle down. Are you okay with taking that risk, or would you like to brainstorm some options right now to see if we can figure out a way for you to make it in today?”

#### 2. Student had a missed day due to an unexcused/unplanned absence
> “I want to talk to you about missing yesterday. Remember, First Shift is an 8-week rapid employment program, and the expectation is that you’re stable enough to be here every day and let us know in advance if something is scheduled. I understand yesterday felt like an emergency, and you called in—so it’s not a No Call/No Show—but it does count as an unplanned absence. Under First Shift rules, **two or more unplanned absences** means you’ll have to step back into **Reentry Navigation** to get stabilized. Let’s look at what caused yesterday’s absence so we can put a plan in place and make sure it doesn’t happen again.”

---

### 4.2 No Call / No Show (NCNS)
*(Original: Guide p. 74 | First Shift Rule: **ANY No Call/No Show (NCNS)** is a Stability Issue → moved out of First Shift back into Reentry Navigation [or exited/terminated]; override requires Program Director / Executive Director.)*

#### 1. First NCNS
> “I wanted to explain what a No Call/No Show means for employers and for First Shift. If someone misses more than 15 minutes of a scheduled day without calling and speaking to staff or leaving a voicemail, that is a NCNS. Employers fire people on the spot for that, and in First Shift, **any single No Call/No Show** is a stability issue. Because First Shift is an 8-week rapid employment track, our policy is that a NCNS moves you out of First Shift and back into **Reentry Navigation** so we can figure out what got in the way and help you stabilize before you try First Shift again. I totally understand that something may have come up—let’s talk about what happened and what you need in Reentry Navigation right now.”

#### 2. Pattern of NCNSs / Re-engagement
> “I wanted to check in with you. You’ve had multiple missed days and No Call/No Shows, and I hate to see you spinning your wheels. First Shift requires consistent daily stability for 8 weeks, so we can’t keep you in the First Shift cohort right now. We’re going to move you back to **Reentry Navigation** [or exit you to **Resource Coordination** if you aren’t able to come in consistently] so you have time to stabilize whatever is keeping you from showing up. Once those barriers are handled, we can talk about whether you’re ready to re-enter First Shift.”

---

### 4.3 Tardiness
*(Original: Guide p. 75–76 | First Shift Rule: Must call in; tardiness is addressed in case management. If tardiness stems from ongoing stability barriers [transportation, childcare, housing, court] or turns into missed days/2+ unplanned absences, it triggers a move back to Reentry Navigation.)*

#### 1. Phone call conversation to student reporting in late
> “Thanks for giving me a call. Calling if you’re going to be late is exactly what we’re looking for—good job. How late are you going to be? *[less than 15 min]* Okay, what exact time will you be here? *[get commitment]* Alright, we’ll see you at [time].”

#### 2. First conversation for being late
> “Thanks for calling yesterday to let me know you were running late. Why do you think calling to let me know is so important? Let’s talk today or tomorrow about any issues affecting your ability to get here on time—like transportation or morning schedule—so it doesn’t turn into missed days or affect your spot in First Shift. Sound good?”

#### 3. Second / pattern conversation for being late
> “Hey, I want to talk with you about being late again yesterday. What happened? *[listen to student]* I know stuff comes up, and that’s why we’re working together—we want your life running smoothly so you can hold down the job we place you in. What do you think will happen in the short term if you keep showing up late? What will happen in the long run—both with employers and with your spot in First Shift? What are some concrete things you can do to be on time? Next time [X] happens, how can you handle it so it doesn’t set you back?”

#### 4. Exit / Move to Reentry Navigation conversation for persistent tardiness
> “Let’s talk again about you being late. Like we already talked about, a pattern of being late is a major problem for First Shift. One of the promises we make to our employer partners is that you’re going to show up on time every day, and we only have 8 weeks to get you ready. Because your [transportation / housing / childcare / schedule] isn’t stable enough right now to get here on time consistently, we need to step you back out of First Shift and into **Reentry Navigation** while we get that worked out. Once you’re stabilized and ready to be on time daily, we can look at getting you back into First Shift.”

### 4.4 Illegal Drug Use (Drug Testing & Breathalyzer)
*(Original: Guide p. 78 | First Shift Rules: **THC only** with no active on-site use/impairment/negative impact may remain active; **hard substances** [meth, cocaine, opiates] generally result in **termination/exit**; **refusal, leaving before testing, or tampering** = **immediate termination**; **failed drug test/breathalyzer** is a Stability Issue.)*

#### 1. New student testing positive for marijuana (THC) at enrollment
> “We know that you’re testing positive for marijuana today. Staying drug-free is a program expectation—especially because most of the employers we place you with drug test—but under our First Shift guidelines, if THC is the only thing showing up and you aren’t coming in impaired or smelling like weed, you can stay active in the program while it clears your system. Let’s set a clean date today based on the last time you smoked and track your progress. Does that sound okay?”

#### 2. Tests positive for hard substances (cocaine, meth, opiates) with strong history of drug abuse
> “You tested positive for [cocaine/meth/opiates] today on the drug test. Substance use has been a big issue for you in the past and creates an ongoing risk for you and for job placement. Under First Shift policy, hard-substance use like this means we have to exit you from the program. We don’t specialize in intensive drug treatment here, so we want to connect you through **Resource Coordination** to a specialized treatment center like the Charleston Center so you can get the right support.”

#### 3. Tests positive for hard substances (isolated incident / lifestyle factor)
> “Let’s talk about your positive drug test today. In your assessment you didn’t talk about [cocaine/etc.] being a regular problem for you—what’s going on? *[Listen and assess whether it is an isolated incident or ongoing risk.]* Because this is a hard substance, our standard policy is program termination. *[If Leadership/PM determines an isolated incident exception applies under close supervision:]* For today, you’re being sent home, and you’ll need to meet with me before returning under close supervision—any further positive test will result in immediate exit from First Shift. *[Otherwise:]* Because of this failed test, we have to exit you from First Shift today and can refer you to **Reentry Navigation / Resource Coordination** for recovery support.”

#### 4. Continues to test positive for marijuana after 30 days of enrollment
> “We need to talk about your drug test results. You’re still testing positive for marijuana and it’s been over 30 days. What’s going on? *[‘I smoked a lot so it’s taking a long time to leave my system.’]* Here’s the reality: First Shift is an 8-week rapid employment program, and we’re already halfway through. Most of our employer partners drug test before hiring, and continuing to test positive for THC is going to block your job placement options. Let’s talk about whether you’re actually stopped smoking and what your placement plan looks like.”

#### 5. *(First Shift Addition)* Drug Test Refusal, Leaving Before Testing, or Tampering (Temperature Out of Range)
> **Refusal / Leaving:** “Refusing a drug screen or leaving the building before giving a sample counts as a failed test under First Shift policy and results in immediate termination from the program.”
>
> **Temperature Out of Range:** “I need to pull you aside privately for a second. The temperature on this sample is out of range, which indicates potential tampering. I want to give you a chance to be upfront with me about what’s going on. If you’re honest with me right now, we can do an immediate retest—though you can’t leave the building or go back to class until we finish it. If you aren’t willing to be honest about it, we’ll have to exit you from the program today.”

### 4.5 Smelling Like Marijuana
*(Original: Guide p. 80 | First Shift Rule: **Smelling like marijuana or alcohol** is explicitly a **Stability Issue** → criteria for moving someone out of First Shift back into Reentry Navigation; on-site impairment = immediate removal leading to suspension or exit.)*

#### 1. Student arrives at the center smelling like marijuana
> “Hey man, what’s up? You smell like marijuana. You know you can’t be in class or in the center smelling like that—it’s risky for other folks trying to stay sober and it’s against First Shift expectations. Let’s step aside and do a drug screen right now to see where things stand.”

#### 2. Student smells like marijuana and tests positive / appears impaired
> “Your test is coming back positive [or you appear impaired], and you smell like marijuana today. I’m going to have to send you home right now—we can’t have you in the center today. Let’s talk tomorrow when you’re sober about next steps.”

#### 3. Next-day meeting after being sent home for smelling like marijuana
> “Let’s talk about yesterday. One of the core expectations of First Shift is staying drug-free and not coming into the center smelling like marijuana or alcohol. Under First Shift rules, **smelling like marijuana** is a stability issue, which means we have to step you out of First Shift and move you back into **Reentry Navigation** [unless a Program Director/Executive Director override is approved]. We still want to work with you and help you get stable, so let’s talk about what’s going on at home or with your rides that led to this and build a plan in Reentry Navigation.”

### 4.6 Smelling Like Alcohol
*(Original: Guide p. 81–82 | First Shift Rule: **Smelling like marijuana or alcohol** is a **Stability Issue** → moved out of First Shift back into Reentry Navigation; on-site impairment / drinking during program hours = immediate removal leading to suspension or termination/exit.)*

#### 1. Student smells like alcohol
> “Hey man, can I talk to you for a minute? Listen, I can smell alcohol on you. What’s going on? *[‘I drank too much last night.’]* Well, we can’t have you in the center since you smell like alcohol. You need to go home for today, and we’ll talk about it tomorrow when you’re sober.”

#### 2. Next-day meeting after being asked to leave for smelling like alcohol
> “Let’s talk about you being sent home yesterday. The expectation in First Shift is that you never come in smelling like alcohol—on a job site, that gets you fired immediately. Under First Shift policy, **smelling like alcohol** is a stability issue, so we have to move you out of First Shift and back into **Reentry Navigation** to address what’s going on [unless an override is granted by leadership]. Let’s talk through why that happened and what support you need so it doesn’t happen again.”

#### 3. Next-day meeting after being intoxicated / drinking during break at the center
> “Let’s talk about you being sent home yesterday. Drinking or being intoxicated here at the center is an immediate safety and stability violation. We won’t be able to keep you in First Shift. We’re going to exit you from the program today, and through **Resource Coordination** we can connect you with a substance use treatment program like the Charleston Center.”

### 4.7 Resistance / Discord
*(Original: Guide p. 83–84 | First Shift Context: 8-week rapid timeline—cannot extend program length or wait 4+ weeks of non-engagement. Persistent unwillingness to engage in CBT/Case Management/Briefcase means stepping back to Reentry Navigation or exiting.)*

#### 1. Rolling with resistance in early case management session
> “Today I want your help to create a plan that we’ll use to guide our weekly case management sessions during these 8 weeks of First Shift. It’s our roadmap for what to talk about and work on together. The plan includes things that have caused you problems in the past connected to legal trouble—some outside of us, like friends or where we live, and some we have complete control over, like our attitude and free time. Sound good? *[okay]* Great, so what types of things you had control over—like your attitude, thinking, or free time—caused you problems?”

#### 2. Giving options of intervention tools
> “One thing I thought we could work on today is something you learned about in class called ‘should’ thinking. Do you remember that lesson? *[yes]* Right, it’s when we think things should or shouldn’t be a certain way and it causes frustration. I’ve noticed that when you talk about your family, probation officer, and support network. Are you okay with working on that today? *[‘No, I’d rather work on something else.’]* Okay, no problem. Another thing you flagged was impulsivity. Which one would you rather work through today—a ‘domino effect’ worksheet or practicing a ‘stop & think’ skill in a role play?”

#### 3. Warning a student about the need for engagement in case management
> “I’ve offered you a few options of things to work on today, but it doesn’t seem like you’re motivated to work through anything. What’s going on? *[‘I just don’t have anything going on that I need help with.’]* Okay, I hear you. Remember, First Shift is an 8-week rapid employment program—we only have a short window to get your Briefcase, readiness gates, and thinking skills where they need to be for an employer. Even when things feel calm, there are always opportunities to practice and get sharper. If you feel like you don’t want to work on the case plan right now, we can step you back to **Reentry Navigation** or give you a referral to a temporary agency through **Resource Coordination**—no harm, no foul. Why don’t you think on it and let’s touch base in a day or two?”

*(Original: Guide p. 85 | First Shift Context: Sleeping/severe fatigue is flagged on the Health & Wellness Observation Screen and Stability Checks. No 16-week "switching shifts" or extending program; if due to medical/mental health/instability, address via Health Screen/referrals or step back to Reentry Navigation.)*

#### 1. First conversation about sleeping in class during check-in
> “I saw that you fell asleep in class the other day. What’s going on? *(Check for underlying medical/fatigue, second job, or stability issues.)*”

#### 2. Second conversation about sleeping in class
> “I noticed you fell asleep in class again this week. Last time we talked, you said you worked late and it wouldn’t happen again. What happened this time? *[‘I was up late with my kid.’]* Okay, I get that things happen and sitting in class can be tough. Let’s look at what’s causing the exhaustion—whether we need to adjust your routine or check in on any health or stability barriers—and pick some strategies you can use next time you start feeling sleepy in class.”

#### 3. Third conversation about sleeping in class
> “I saw that you fell asleep in class again. What do you think is going to happen in the short term if you keep falling asleep here? What will happen in the long run? This is a 911. In First Shift, we only have 8 weeks to prove to employers that you’re ready to work a full shift without nodding off. If you can’t stay awake and participate in class—or if there’s a medical, housing, or schedule issue behind it—we’ll have to step you back into **Reentry Navigation** until that’s resolved so it doesn’t cost you a job placement.”

### 4.9 Leaving Early
*(Original: Guide p. 87 | First Shift Rule: No 2-week program extension. Leaving early without 24-hour permission is an indicator of instability; 2+ unplanned absences or leaving early due to stability barriers [childcare, housing, transportation, health] = moved back to Reentry Navigation [or exited/terminated if repeated or during a drug test].)*

#### 1. Student asks to leave early for a family emergency
> “It sounds like you feel like you need to leave because of a family emergency. Remember, leaving on the spot is impulsive behavior and causes problems on a real job. You can make your own decision here, but leaving right now is not approved since it’s a last-minute request. Are you okay with brainstorming some options on how to handle the situation from here? Remember, First Shift is an 8-week program, and unplanned absences or leaving mid-day—especially if it happens more than once—counts as a stability issue that can move you back into **Reentry Navigation**.”

#### 2. Student leaves early without notifying staff
> “Let’s talk about you leaving in the middle of the day yesterday. *[‘I told X student to tell you I had an emergency.’]* Right, he told me, but telling another student isn’t notifying staff. What do you think happens if you walk off a job without telling your supervisor? *[‘I’ll get fired.’]* Exactly. Leaving without talking to staff is a serious stability and accountability issue. In First Shift, we can’t have that happen again—if you have another unplanned absence or walk-out, our policy is to move you out of First Shift and back into **Reentry Navigation** until things are stable enough for you to complete the 8 weeks.”

### 4.10 Aggressiveness Towards Staff
*(Original: Guide p. 88–89 | First Shift Rule: Physical violence = **termination**; inability to safely regulate behavior in a group/center setting is a **Stability Issue** → immediate removal / exit or move back to Reentry Navigation rather than "extending program length".)*

#### 1. Student is escalating towards aggression
> “Hey man, now is a good time to use some of the skills you’ve been learning in class. What self-control strategies could help you stay calm right here? Remember you can take deep breaths, count backwards, step outside for a break, or use some new thinking.”

#### 2. Student makes a verbal or physical threat to staff
> “Let’s take a break and step away right now. We’ll talk again once things are calm [or have leadership follow up regarding termination/exit].”

### 4.11 Aggressiveness Towards Others
*(Original: Guide p. 90 | First Shift Rule: Cannot safely regulate behavior = Stability Issue / exit or move back to Reentry Navigation; no "extending program length".)*

#### 1. Student on the phone yelling
> “Hey man, time out. It’s not okay to be yelling on the phone here at the center. *[student ends call]* Let’s practice some self-control strategies right now to calm down.”

#### 2. Student yelling on the phone with his girlfriend (doesn’t respond and keeps yelling)
> “Hey man, time out. It’s not okay to be yelling on the phone. *[student ignores warning and keeps yelling]* Please end the call—you’re using aggressive communication right now, which isn’t okay. *[if call doesn’t end]* I’m going to give you two choices: you can end the call now and come inside so we can talk about it and get you back on track, or you can leave for the day—and remember, leaving unplanned counts as an absence that puts your First Shift spot at risk.”

### 4.12 Passive Aggressiveness
*(Original: Guide p. 91–92)*

#### 1. Passive aggressive communication, responds pro-socially
> **Student:** “Oh awesome, another drug test. Trying to catch us up again.”  
> **Staff:** “I just want to remind you that the expectation in First Shift is to stay positive.”  
> **Student:** “My bad. I was just kidding, sorry about that.”

#### 2. Passive aggressive communication, doesn’t respond pro-socially
> **Student:** “Oh awesome, another drug test. Trying to catch us up again.”  
> **Staff:** “I just want to remind you that the expectation in First Shift is to stay positive.”  
> **Student:** “Whatever. It’s all bullshit anyway.”  
> **Staff:** “Now is a good time to practice one of the skills you’ve been learning in class. What about managing frustration?”  
> **Student:** “Nah, I’m good.”  
> **Staff:** “Well listen, I want you to stay for the rest of the day and finish your work, but the expectation is to stay positive and practice skills. You can stay and be positive, or you can leave today—and keep in mind that leaving today counts as an unplanned absence toward the two-absence limit in First Shift. What do you think?”

### 4.13 Poor Classroom and/or Work Readiness Performance
*(Original: Guide p. 93–94 | First Shift Context: Replaces "in-house print shop / floor manager" with First Shift's **CBT classroom, Skillcat, Briefcase, and Readiness Gates**—or **TJ** if in Transitional Jobs. If performance doesn't improve, participant is not ready for job placement and is moved back to **Reentry Navigation**, **TJ** [if appropriate/open], or **terminated**.)*

#### 1. Attitude issue (with facilitator / staff)
> “I checked in with [facilitator/staff] about how things are going, and they said they had to pull you aside to talk on Friday. What was that about? *[‘I was just having a bad day. We worked it out.’]* That’s good—attitude is one of the biggest predictors of keeping a job. When we recommend you to our employer partners at the end of these 8 weeks, one of the things they’re counting on is that you can handle frustration and work things out professionally. Let’s talk about what triggered that situation and how you could’ve handled it better so it doesn’t affect your performance. Sound okay?”

#### 2. Phone use during class / lab / Briefcase time
> “I checked in with [facilitator/staff] and they said they’ve had to talk to you a few times about being on your phone during class and lab time. What’s going on with that? *[‘I’ve just had a lot of stuff going on at home.’]* Okay, we can talk about that in a minute. First, what do you think happens in the short run if you keep using your phone during work or class? What happens in the long run—both with finishing First Shift and keeping a job once we place you? Let’s figure out how you can handle what’s going on at home without putting your First Shift spot or your future job at risk. Sound okay?”

#### 3. Poor effort in CBT activities, Skillcat, and role plays
> “I checked in with [facilitator] about how things are going in the classroom, and he said he’s talked to you a few times lately about putting your head down and not participating in discussions or Skillcat/Briefcase work. What’s going on? *[‘I get tired. It’s boring in there sometimes.’]* Okay, I get that. But your effort in class, Skillcat, and your Briefcase gates is all we have to go on when we’re deciding if you’re ready for job placement in 8 weeks. If you aren’t ready for rapid employment, we’ll have to step you back to **Reentry Navigation** or look at **Transitional Jobs** instead of placing you. What do you need to do to get your effort up to 100%?”

#### 4. Complaining / negative attitude in class
> “I checked in with [facilitator] about how things are going in the classroom, and he said he’s talked to you a few times lately about complaining about homework and in-class work. What’s going on? *[‘I’m just messing with him.’]* Okay, remember—if you have a complaint, use your skill and talk to him or me in private, okay? Saying negative things about the work in front of the group makes it seem like you’re frustrated and sets a bad example for the other five guys in your cohort. Do you want to practice using assertive communication or making a complaint right now—which one?”
