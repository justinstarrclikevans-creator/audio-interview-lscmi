# Turn90 Facilitator Scoring Guide
## Official 20-Item Classroom Facilitation Evaluation Standard

> **Turn90 QA Standard:** Criteria for evaluating facilitator adherence to Evidence-Based Cognitive Behavioral Training, Modeling Neutrality, Classroom Rule Enforcement, Roleplay Setup, and Group Engagement.

---

## Facilitation Evaluation Rubric

### Yes/No Basics (not scored in total?)
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Yes |
| **Good (4)** | No |

### On time for class
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | At front of the class prior to start; ready to start class on time |
| **Good (4)** | Not at front of class prior to start time; not ready to start class at start time |

---

### Reviews class rules and expectations
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Reviews classroom rules and expectations in 2 minutes or less |
| **Good (4)** | Does not review classroom rules and expectations; or takes longer than 2 minutes |

### Classroom is ready
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Appropriate classroom setup with enough chairs and desks, clean, tidy, tables wiped, floor free of trash, trash empty, no food containers, board is clean, smells clean, clock is visible and accurate, skills hung on walls |
| **Good (4)** | Missing one item, for example, trash can has not been empty or missing a chair |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Missing two items |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Missing three items |

### Prepared for class
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | All class materials are printed out with enough copies made in advance; audio / visual materials are prepared and working; board activities are prepared in advance (when it makes sense); markers are fresh and ready; skills are prepped; role play props are available when needed; materials prepared to review homework; point board is updated; helpful cheat sheets or additional activity materials is provided; feeling faces / coping skills on hand / on display to assist with discussion. |
| **Good (4)** | One small issue, but does not need to leave classroom. For example, all class materials are printed out with enough copies made in advance; audio / visual materials are prepared and working; markers tested out in advance; board ready to go; board activities / definitions could have been prepared in advance but were not; role play props or skills could have been prepared in advance but were not; materials prepared to review homework; point board is updated |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Two small issues but does not need to leave classroom, for example: All class materials are printed out with enough copies made in advance; audio / visual materials are prepared and working; audio / visual materials are prepped but time delay in looking for them or some small technical issue causes a delay but they still work; points board updated before class begins; need to locate markers or role play props or skills for role plays but does not need to leave the classroom |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Three small issues OR need to leave the classroom for any prep reason. For example, leave the classroom for a classroom supply or handouts; audio / visual materials are prepared but not working; points board is not updated before class begins (score poor if any of the above are met) |

### Follows lesson plan
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Has lesson plan and follows it with no room for improvement; stays on track with lesson and completes all activities as directed |
| **Good (4)** | Has lesson plan and follows it with some small areas for improvement; misses minor points within lesson; delivers key activity as intended; conversation remains on track |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Has lesson plan, says key concepts and facilitates activities but misses some minor part of the discussion or activity or minor part of the lesson plan is not followed as instructed; conversation is offtrack for short periods but able to bring conversation back to lesson within reasonable timeframe |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Has lesson plan; says key concepts; conducts key activities but does not deliver the model situation and / or main activity as intended; difficulty in keeping conversation on track |

### Enderstands core concepts
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Follows the script while still being able to fully explain the concepts in personal words and experiences; spontaneously taught the concept using additional appropriate and effective examples; connects concepts of the lesson to other lessons and examples previously discussed. |
| **Good (4)** | Follows the script while still being able to fully explain the concepts in personal words and experiences; offered different explanations to teach the key concepts; was able to fully answer all questions with thoughtful responses that indicated a thorough understanding of the core concept. Good is scored rather than very good if facilitator did not use additional appropriate examples and did not connect concepts to other lessons or examples. |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Follows the script while still being able to fully explain the concepts in personal words and experiences; Acceptable is scored rather than good when concept was explained adequately but the facilitator did not did not offer different explanations to teach key concepts, did not have thoughtful responses to all questions about key concepts, did not use additional examples or connect concepts to other lessons or examples. |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Was able to explain the concept via the script only; follows the script and ask scripted questions but struggled to present any additional examples or explanations in teaching key concepts when needed |

### Makes appropriate use of class time
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Good pacing for each section; tracks time during class; completes main activity for each participant; ends on time; makes productive use of time if class ends early; has a backup plan if class ends early; adjusts timing for each section if number of trainees is less than 7 or more than 9 |
| **Good (4)** | Good pacing for each section; may or may not track time during class; uses time well at the end of class but unclear if there is a specific backup plan v. spur of the moment plan |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Completes all parts of lesson; makes up time during later sections for time managemenet issues earlier in the lesson but is able to conduct main activity effectively; utilizes any extra class time with constructive conversation; |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Has multiple times management issues, such as completes all parts of the lesson but spends too much on one section which causes too little time on main activity or rushing; time is not tracked during class; conversation at the end is unproductive. If all main activities are not completed for any reason, give a score of poor or very poor depending on additional time management problems. |

### Enthusiastic about material
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Score very good if all of the following are met: Clearly spent time prepping and considering why the class is important to the group and to the facilitator personally; Shows genuine excitement for the lesson; conveys why the topic is personally important; brings energy and enthusiasm to the topic; enthusiasm remains for the duration of the lesson not just as an opening statement; is able to get the group interested in the topic based on energy and enthusiasm; is able to connect topic to values and interests of the group to raise enthusiasm and interest in learning "this is how this skill will help you X, Y or Z..." |
| **Good (4)** | Score good if all of the following are met: Shows genuine excitement for the lesson; conveys why the topic is personally important; brings energy and enthusiasm to the topic; enthusiasm remains for the duration of the lesson not just as an opening statement; is able to get the group interested in the topic based on energy and enthusiasm |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Score acceptable if all of the following are met: Shows genuine excitement for the lesson; brings energy and enthusiams to the topic; is able to get the group interested in the topic based on energy and enthusiasm |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Score poor if the following are met: Shows genuine excitement for the lesson but does not remain excited for the class duration; is unable to translate excitement to get the group interested; energy and enthusiasm diminishes over the class; shows excitement but it is mediocre or does not feel entirely genuine |

### Homework review is well executed
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Utilizes the point system effectively; records points effectively for being on time and homework completion minimizing attention to point loss; does not give credit for homework if not fully completed; uses visual check when necessary; manages time effectively during homework check-in ensuring it does not exceed 2 minutes per person or 20 minutes total; gives feedback when appropriate. A score of very good is given when all of the above criteria are met and there is no room for improvement. |
| **Good (4)** | Score as acceptable if there is one minor mistake or area for improvement. Do not score good if facilitator went over in time. |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Score acceptable if the facilitator had no problems except went over in time OR had two minor mistakes but met time constraints. |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Score poor if the facilitator went over time and had one other mistake OR met time expectations but had more than two areas for improvement. |

### Main activity / role pays are setup up and executed well
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Every role play or main activity situation was appropriate for the main actor, the role play was set up effectively, facilitator explained situation to the group, co-actor knew the part they were playing, there was shared understanding of how the role play would be acted out and how it would end / how to role play the final "action," any deviation from agreed upon role play was stopped and restarted, role play / main acitivity was not forced if resistance appeared faciliator pivoted effectively, the main activity was completed for each person, constructive feedback was provided when it would add value. A score of "very good" here is met when all of the above were met for all role plays and there were no issues during the main activity portion of the lesson. |
| **Good (4)** | The majority of role plays or main activity situations were appropriate for the main actor, the role play was set up effectively, facilitator explained situation to the group, co-actor knew the part they were playing, there was shared understanding of how the role play would be acted out and how it would end / how to role play the final "action," any deviation from agreed upon role play was stopped and restarted, role play / main acitivity was not forced if resistance appeared faciliator pivoted effectively, the main activity was completed for each person, constructive feedback was provided when it would add value. A person gets a "good" score here when everyone completed a role play with sufficient time and there were two or less role plays that had any minor issue noted above. |
| **Acceptable (3)** | The majority of role plays or main activity situations were appropriate for the main actor, the role play was set up effectively, facilitator explained situation to the group, co-actor knew the part they were playing, there was shared understanding of how the role play would be acted out and how it would end / how to role play the final "action," any deviation from agreed upon role play was stopped and restarted, role play / main acitivity was not forced if resistance appeared faciliator pivoted effectively, the main activity was completed for each person, constructive feedback was provided when it would add value. A person gets a "acceptable" score here as long almost all of the above were met and everyone completed a role play with sufficient time and there less than half of the group that had any of the above issues. If there are 8 people in group, all completed their role plays with enough time, but 4 had some minor issue noted above, they would receive "acceptable." |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Multiple role plays or main activity situations were not appropriate for the main actor, the role plays were not set up effectively, facilitator consistently did not explain situation to the group, co-actor did not know the part they were playing, there was not shared understanding of how the role play would be acted out and how it would end / how to role play the final "action," any deviation from agreed upon role play was not stopped and restarted, role play / main acitivity was forced if resistance appeared faciliator did not pivoted effectively, the main activity was not completed for each person, constructive feedback was not provided when it would add value. A person gets a "poor" if all people did not complete a main activity with sufficient time, if many of the role plays experienced problems noted above or multiple major issues indicating a lack of prepartion to facilitate main activity or lack of understanding of content. |

### Models behavior asked of participants
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Modeled prosocial behavior at all times, i.e. did not exhibit anti-social or criminal attitudes or past examples of such; did not make any inappropriate or derogatory comments about groups, Turn90, coworkers, rules, police, probation, etc.; brought positive attitude and energy to class; stayed calm during any problematic and conflict situations; admitted mistakes and apologized when appropriate; did not do things that are not allowed for students in the classroom (ex: eating or phone use); was prepared for class on time and with all needed materials; used “I” statements (“I feel disrespected when you talk while I am teaching class”) when necessary; |
| **Good (4)** | Modeled prosocial behavior at all times, i.e. did not exhibit anti-social or criminal attitudes or past examples of such; did not make any inappropriate or derogatory comments about groups, Turn90, coworkers, rules, police, probation, etc.; brought positive attitude and energy to class; stayed calm during any problematic and conflict situations; admitted mistakes and apologized when appropriate; did not do things that are not allowed for students in the classroom (ex: eating or phone use); did not complain about job, program, expectations, etc. Still some room to grow when using "I" statements and in class prepardness (a person may not be able to get the A/V to work or missing handout copies and still get "good" here). |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Modeled prosocial behavior at all times, i.e. did not exhibit anti-social or criminal attitudes or past examples of such; did not make any inappropriate or derogatory comments about groups, Turn90, coworkers, rules, police, probation, etc.; was positive but at times felt flustered, overwhelmed, frustrated, or unsure ; stayed calm during any problematic and conflict situations but did not always handle them correctly; did not do things that are not allowed for students in the classroom (ex: eating or phone use); still room to grow when admitting mistakes, apologizing, being prepared; saying "I don't know" etc. |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | One incident of not modeling prosocial behavior i.e. exhibited anti-social or criminal attitudes or past examples, such as making any inappropriate or derogatory comments about groups, Turn90, coworkers, rules, police, probation, etc.; was positive but at times felt flustered, overwhelmed, frustrated, or unsure; did not stay totally calm during any problematic and conflict situations; exhibited any behavior not allowed for students in the classroom (ex: eating or phone use); |

### Engages group effectively
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Engages with all participants in different ways; works hard and creatively to include each person in dialogue; does not get caught up in one-on-one discussions; does not allow two or three participants to dominate conversation; diverts away from one person speaking too much when necessary; uses silence effectively to engage; asks questions different ways to get the entire group involved or re-clarify the question; calls on individuals when needed with specific reference to past comments to engage them |
| **Good (4)** | Engages with all participants in different ways; works hard and creatively to include each person in dialogue; does not get caught up in one-on-one discussions; does not allow two or three participants to dominate conversation; diverts away from one person speaking too much when necessary; |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Makes effort to engage all participants in the discussion; makes some attempt to redirect questions when discussion is dominated; catches one-on-one conversations ("dating") and does not re-engage |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Does not make special effort to engage all participants; spends too much time allowing one or more participants to dominate the conversation; attempts to redirect but is unsuccessful |

### Consistently enforces classroom rules and expectations
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Utilizes the point system effectively; records points in real time for being on time, homework and uses out of class passes; addresses any rule breaking behavior effectively by giving proper warning(s) and escalating intervention; asks participant to leave if not following rule after multiple attempts, uses private conversations effectively if needed (before / during / or after class); uses effective core correctional practices use of authority ("I'd love for you to stay, you worked hard to be here today, are you able to..."); minimizes attention to point loss, does not give credit for homework if not fully completed; intervenes effectively when eyes closed / head down by moving towards the person, leaning down, etc; |
| **Good (4)** | Utilizes the point system effectively; records points in real time for being on time, homework and uses out of class passes; addresses any rule breaking behavior effectively by giving proper warning(s) and escalating intervention; asks participant to leave if not following rule after multiple attempts; minimizes attention to point loss, does not give credit for homework if not fully completed; intervenes when eyes closed / head down |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Utilizes the point system effectively; records points in real time for being on time, homework and uses out of class passes; addresses rule breaking behavior eventually, but it may take multiple infractions for the facilitator to finally intervene; the facilitator may or may not follow through on asking a person to leave; does not give credit for homework completely unfinished but may overlook if a person does not have it 100% completed (missing a few questions) |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Inconsistent in using the point system; records points but may not be in real time; addresses rule breaking behavior inconsistently; the facilitator does not follow through on consequences after intervening; gives credit for homework even though it's clear the person has not completed it but only reported on it verbally; a person has their eyes closed intermittenly but the facilitator does not address it in any way |

### Makes good use of physical presence and space
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Does not facilitate with back towards group; minimizes time spent charting or writing on board during facilitation or group participation; does not ask a question and then turn back toward person(s) speaking unless quickly to chart answer / only party turned; walks around room to engage full participation; walks towards distractions to minimize them; uses hand gestures and eye contact to create interest and engagement; does not sit during class (unless medical reason); does not stand idle in one place; moves to the back or side of the room to watch role plays; kneels down to assist with in class work; moves about room (uses visual confirmation) to ensure homework is completed entirely and in-class work is being done accurately |
| **Good (4)** | Does not facilitate with back towards group; minimizes time spent charting or writing on board during facilitation or group participation; does not ask a question and then turn back toward person(s) speaking unless quickly to chart answer / only party turned; walks around room to engage full participation; walks towards distractions to minimize them; uses hand gestures and eye contact to create interest and engagement; does not sit during class at any point; could be more consistent in using visual confirmation on handout / homework completion; could be more consistent in moving about room or moving about to help one-on-one during in-class activities |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Does not facilitate with back towards group; minimizes time spent charting or writing on board during facilitation or group participation; does not ask a question and then turn back toward person(s) speaking unless quickly to chart answer / only party turned; walks around room; does not facilitate from one place in the room; does not sit during class at any point |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | At some time during class facilitated with back towards group; there was unnecessary time spent charting or writing on board during facilitation or group participation; ask at least one question and then turn back toward person(s) speaking; minimally walked around room; sat down during class at any point |

### Uses praise effectively
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Facilitator is on a constant lookout for times to give praise; praise is tailored to the individual and focused on demonstration of problematic to acceptable behavior; facilitator describes the positive behavior while giving praise ("you really stepped it up today with your homework because..." rather than "great job on your homework today"); a variety of different statements are used to praise - facilitator did not overuse one statement ("great job"); praise was connected to future accomplishments ("getting here early really shows a lot of focus which is going to help you out on the next job"); praise is connected to effort and ability ("your homework was right on point. I can see you put a lot of effort into that") |
| **Good (4)** | Facilitator gives praise consistently throughout class; but not always tailored to the individual but instead praise is given to all participants to reinforce all behavior; some of the time the facilitator follows the best practices in giving praise, i.e. describes the positive behavior while giving praise ("you really stepped it up today with your homework because..." rather than "great job on your homework today"); and uses a variety of different statements are used to praise - facilitator did not overuse one statement ("great job"); praise was connected to future accomplishments ("getting here early really shows a lot of focus which is going to help you out on the next job"); praise is connected to effort and ability ("your homework was right on point. I can see you put a lot of effort into that") |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Praise is given regularly throughout class but it is not often tailored to the individual (i.e. the facilitator does not look specifically to reinforce behavior a person has been struggling with but instead targeted to all people for all positive behavior); facilitator struggles to vary praise and follow all the best practices but sometimes connects praise to effort, describes the behavior; and varies the praise statements |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Praise is given occassionally but without clear plan or direction on who to praise, for what and in what way |

Very good is given here if facilitator uses 7 or more variations of positive affirmations / gives 20 or more affirmations during class (but does not overuse one significantly) |

Good is given here if facilitator uses 5 or 6 variations of positive affirmations / gives 15+ affirmations during class (but does not overuse one significantly) |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Facilitator uses a variety of positive affirmations to signal approval; must vary affirmations used throughout class showing an understanding that variety of words more impactful as a motivator. Acceptable is given here if facilitator uses 3 or 4 variations of positive affirmations / gives 10 or more affirmations during class (but does not overuse one significantly) |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Facilitator uses positive affirmations sparingly (5-10 times) and does not use a variety of affirming words (1-2) or uses a lot of affirmations but significantly overuses the same word to the point it is not effective as an affirmation (uses the same word 10+ times a class). |

A rating of good is given here if facilitator uses 5 or 6 variations of nonverbal expressions of approval and uses nonverbal expressions of approval 15-20 times during class. |

A rating of acceptable is given here if facilitator uses at least 3 variations of nonverbal expressions of approval and uses nonverbal expressions of approval approximately 10 times during class. |

### Uses reflective listening effectively
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Facilitator uses a variety of reflections regularly throughout the class; must vary types of reflections used throughout class showing an understanding of simple and complex reflections and when each should be used. Simple reflections are repeating back what a person has said and slightly rephrasing what a person has said. They are used at times to acknowledge particiation and keep the conversation going as it is. Facilitator demonstrates ability to use complex reflections to take the conversation deeper, to prevent the conversation from going around in circles, to take a guess as to what a person is trying to say or to add meaning to their words, or wanting to guide the conversation towards change. Examples of complex reflections: paraphrasing, using a metaphor, describe the feeling, use double sided change talk, or continue the sentence where the person left off. Very good is given here if the facilitator demonstrates mastery of simple and complex reflections. |
| **Good (4)** | Facilitator uses a variety of reflections regularly throughout the class; must vary types of reflections used throughout class showing an understanding of simple and complex refelctions and when each should be used. Good is given if the facilitator uses both simple and complex reflections. Simply reflections are repeating back what a person has said and slightly rephrasing what a person has said. They are used at times to acknowledge participation and keep the conversation going as it is. Facilitator demonstrates ability to use complex reflections to take the conversation deeper, to prevent the conversation from going around in circles, to take a guess as to what a person is trying to say or to add meaning to their words, or wanting to guide the conversation towards change. Examples of complex reflections: paraphrasing, using a metaphor, describe the feeling, use a double sided change talk, or continue to the sentence where the person left off.  Good is given rather than very good if the facilitator missed opportunities to use complex reflections, used simple when complex would have been more effective, and is not fully able to use a strong variety of reflections depending on the goal. |
| **Acceptable (3)** | Facilitator uses simple and complex reflections during class. Acceptable is given here if the facilitator used both simple and complex reflections during class, however, the use of complex reflections were infrequent and not varied in type of reflection used and/or the use of reflections were missplaced. For example, the facilitator used three complex reflections but all were paraphrasing. Or, the facilitator used simple reflections when complex would have been more appropriate to take the conversation deeper or vice versa. There was limited use of more advanced reflective listening. |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Facilitator uses simple reflections appropriately to acknowledge participation and keep the conversation going but does not use any complex reflections during class to guide conversation. |

### Uses questions effectively
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | A score of very good is given if the facilitator demonstrates mastery in all of the below criteria. The facilitator understands why each question is being asked: discussion, buy-in, or looking for a specific answer.  For example, they spend enough time on buy-in questions and limited time on specific questions. Facilitator knows the reason s/he is asking a question and never asks a question without reaching the answer (i.e. never asks "anything else?" without an answer in mind). Questions are asked with an end in mind. Demonstrates an ability to ask questions in multiple ways if necessary to achieve the goal, i.e does not continue to repeat the question asked in the same way if the answer is not being given. Understands when to "double barrell" a question with a follow-up; does not ask follow-up questions without a purpose. Avoids asking questions that take the conversation off track or ask questions of someone who is already dominating the conversation. Uses questions effectively to engage those who have not participated ("what does this side of the room think?"). |
| **Good (4)** | A score of good is given if the facilitator demonstrates competency in all the same criteria as very good, however,they are less able to ask effective questions in the moment when asked to facilitate dynmaically / improvisationally. There were missed opportunities for great follow-up questions, As a part of preparation, better questions could have been developed for group or individual specific issues. |
| **Acceptable (3)** | A score of accepable is given if the facilitator demonstrates competency in all the same criteria as very good, however, they make one or two mistakes related to question asking (i.e. they don't ask an appropriate buy-in question or do not ask a question multple ways to get more engagement or ask a follow-up question without a good reason / answer in mind). |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Facilitator had three to five related to question asking (i.e. did not ask appropriate questions, said "come on" instead of asking the question another way, answered own question, asked poor follow-up questions, asked questions about hypothetically, etc.) |

### Gets buy-in effectively
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | The facilitator understands which part of the lesson requires buy-in and asks buy-in questions appropriately; does not move forward without sufficient buy-in; able to approach getting buy-in from a different angle if needed, for example, use questions off script / improv to ask additional questions or connect to different concepts to increase motivation; understanding if buy-in has been lost and can move back to get it again; understands when buy-in has been gained and then moves foward; does not spend too much time in discussion after buy-in has been achieved so as to risk losing it; does not ask participants to engage in main activity if they are not bought in but looks for alternative option. A score of very good is given if the facilitator demonstrates mastery in all of the below criteria with no issues related to buy-in. |
| **Good (4)** | The facilitator understands which part of the lesson requires buy-in and asks buy-in questions appropriately; does not move forward without sufficient buy-in; able to approach getting buy-in from a different angle if needed, for example, use questions off script / improv to ask additional questions or connect to different concepts to increase motivation; understanding if buy-in has been lost and can move back to get it again; understands when buy-in has been gained and then moves foward; does not spend too much time in discussion after buy-in has been achieved so as to risk losing it; does not ask participants to engage in main activity if they are not bought in but looks for alternative option. A score of good indicates proficiency with getting buy-in, but may have room to improve when needed to go off script, or finding specific ways to motivate certain people, or responding to problematic situations where one person does not engage. |
| **Acceptable (3)** | The facilitator understands which part of the lesson requires buy-in and asks buy-in questions appropriately; does not move forward without sufficient buy-in; able to approach getting buy-in from a different angle if needed, for example, use questions off script / improv to ask additional questions or connect to different concepts to increase motivation; understanding if buy-in has been lost and can move back to get it again; understands when buy-in has been gained and then moves foward; does not spend too much time in discussion after buy-in has been achieved so as to risk losing it; does not ask participants to engage in main activity if they are not bought in but looks for alternative option. A score of acceptable indicates an ability to get and keep buy-in, but may struggle with timing - spending too long or short during buy-in, or losing buy-in later and having some issues with going back to get it again, this facilitator is acceptable but may struggle when conditions for buy-in are not perfect. |
| **Poor (2 / 1)** | Facilitator tries to get buy-in but fails and it causus problems during class; the facilitator struggles to identify buy-in, when s/he has it, how long to spend on buy-in sections, asking sufficient buy-in questions, moving forward without full group buy-in, failing to go back if buy-in is lost, losing buy-in due to too much dialogue, asking participants to engage in material they are not motivated to learn. |

### Avoids overdirecting
| Rating | Operational Performance Criteria |
| :---: | :--- |
| **Very Good (5)** | Accepts differing viewpoints (i.e. does not impose personal values on participants); does not “play the expert”; facilitator does not give answers that could have been reached in a question; avoids making too many suggestions; does not put up suggestions on a chart without buy-in; does not tell people what to work on, force people to work on things they don't wan to or how they should think or act.
