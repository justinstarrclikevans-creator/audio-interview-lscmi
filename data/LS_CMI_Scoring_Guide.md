## 1. General Rating Principles & The 3-to-0 Rating Guide

### The 3-to-0 Rating Scale
Certain items require an evaluative rating from 3 to 0 and should be scored according to the following standard criteria:

| Score | Level | Operational Definition | Assessment Benchmark |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **3** | **Satisfactory** | A satisfactory situation with no need for improvement. | Active prosocial functioning; no risk indicators present. |
| **2** | **Relatively Satisfactory** | A relatively satisfactory situation, with some room for improvement. | Generally stable, minor areas for personal growth. |
| **1** | **Relatively Unsatisfactory** | A relatively unsatisfactory situation with a need for improvement. | Documented strain, inconsistency, or moderate risk factor. |
| **0** | **Very Unsatisfactory** | A very unsatisfactory situation with a clear, strong need for improvement. | Chronic instability, active violation, or severe criminogenic deficit. |

> **Assessor Rule:** Always ask: *"Could I provide one or two concrete illustrative examples from the interview or file as the basis for my rating?"*

## 2. Strength Notation Guidelines
Each of the eight General Risk/Need subcomponents requires the assessor to judge whether the area qualifies as an **Area of Strength**.
- **Subcomponent Focus:** Strengths apply to the **subcomponent as a whole**, never to isolated individual items.
- **Protective Function:** A strength indicates positive prosocial functioning that actively buffers or protects against future criminal behavior.
- **Disqualification Rule:** If any item within a subcomponent indicates a significant deficit (e.g. scored 0 or 1 on a 0–3 rating item, or marked **Yes** on an elevated risk item), that subcomponent **cannot** be marked as a Strength.

## 3. Section 1: General Risk / Need Factor Subcomponents

### 1.1 Criminal History
*Evaluates official criminal convictions, youth dispositions, incarceration history, and institutional behavior.*

| Item # | Assessment Item | Scoring Rule & Guidance |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Any prior youth or adult dispositions / convictions? | Mark **Yes** if official record or reliable admission shows at least one prior conviction (excluding minor traffic). |
| **2** | Two or more prior youth or adult dispositions / convictions? | Mark **Yes** if official record documents 2 or more separate conviction events. |
| **3** | Three or more prior youth or adult dispositions / convictions? | Mark **Yes** if official record documents 3 or more separate conviction events. |
| **4** | Three or more present offenses? | Mark **Yes** if current adjudication/sentencing event involves 3 or more distinct offenses/counts. |
| **5** | Arrested / charged under age 16? | Mark **Yes** if first documented arrest or charge occurred before the 16th birthday. |
| **6** | Ever incarcerated upon conviction? | Mark **Yes** if ever sentenced to prison, jail, or juvenile detention facility following conviction. |
| **7** | Ever punished for institutional misconduct / breach? | Mark **Yes** if reprimanded, segregated, or penalized for rules violations while incarcerated or on probation/parole. |
| **8** | Charge laid, probation breached, or parole suspended during prior community supervision? | Mark **Yes** if revoked, breached, or charged with a new offense while under prior conditional supervision. |

---

### 1.2 Education / Employment
*Evaluates school history, current labor market attachment, job stability, coworker relations, and supervisor interactions.*

| Item # | Assessment Item | Scoring Rule & Guidance |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **9** | Currently unemployed? | Mark **Yes** if not currently employed in full-time or stable part-time work. (Turn90 participants in training transition to First Shift employment). |
| **10** | Frequently unemployed? | Mark **Yes** if history shows repetitive job loss, short tenures, or difficulty sustaining employment. |
| **11** | Never employed for a full year continuously? | Mark **Yes** if participant has never held a single job continuously for 12 consecutive months. |
| **12** | Ever fired or suspended from employment? | Mark **Yes** if terminated for cause, attendance, insubordination, or misconduct. |
| **13** | Less than regular high school graduation / GED? | Mark **Yes** if participant has not completed high school diploma or recognized equivalent. |
| **14** | Ever suspended or expelled from school? | Mark **Yes** if formally removed from school for disciplinary reasons. |
| **15** | Participation / Performance rating (0–3) | **3:** Excelling, reliable, proactive. **2:** Average, acceptable. **1:** Marginal, punctuality issues. **0:** Severely disruptive or absent. |
| **16** | Peer relations at work / school (0–3) | **3:** Cooperates well, positive teamwork. **2:** Generally pleasant. **1:** Frequent friction or alienation. **0:** Hostile, combative with peers. |
| **17** | Authority / Boss relations (0–3) | **3:** Respectful, takes feedback constructively. **2:** Compliant. **1:** Resentful, argues. **0:** Blatantly defiant or hostile. |

### 1.3 Family / Marital
*Evaluates domestic relationships, parental stability, criminal history of family/partner, and home environment.*

| Item # | Assessment Item | Scoring Rule & Guidance |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **18** | Dissatisfaction with marital / significant other relationship (0–3) | **3:** Strong mutual support. **2:** Normal minor tensions. **1:** Moderate chronic conflict. **0:** Severe dysfunction, domestic strife, or violence. |
| **19** | Non-rewarding parental / family ties (0–3) | **3:** Warm, encouraging family unit. **2:** Acceptable contact. **1:** Strained or indifferent. **0:** Alienated, hostile, or completely absent. |
| **20** | Criminal family member / partner? | Mark **Yes** if spouse, partner, parent, or close household member has active criminal involvement or conviction record. |
| **21** | History of domestic abuse / child protection involvement? | Mark **Yes** if official record or substantiated report of domestic battery, restraining orders, or protective agency involvement. |

### 1.4 Leisure / Recreation
*Evaluates prosocial use of free time, participation in organized clubs/sports, and hobbies.*

| Item # | Assessment Item | Scoring Rule & Guidance |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **22** | Absence of recent participation in organized activity (0–3) | **3:** Regularly active in organized sports, church, gym, or hobby group. **2:** Occasional participation. **1:** Rare. **0:** Completely unengaged in organized prosocial recreation. |
| **23** | Could make better use of free time (0–3) | **3:** Excellent time management, productive routine. **2:** Generally structured. **1:** Excessive idle time, boredom. **0:** Pervasive idle street time, hanging out in high-risk spots. |

### 1.5 Companions
*Evaluates peer influences, anti-criminal versus criminal acquaintances, and gang involvement.*

| Item # | Assessment Item | Scoring Rule & Guidance |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **24** | A social network largely composed of criminal acquaintances? | Mark **Yes** if peer circle includes multiple individuals currently involved in illegal activity or active justice supervision. |
| **25** | Few anticriminal friends (0–3) | **3:** Extensive circle of prosocial, law-abiding friends. **2:** Moderate prosocial friends. **1:** Very few anticriminal peers. **0:** No law-abiding friends whatsoever. |
| **26** | Few anticriminal acquaintances? | Mark **Yes** if participant has fewer than three prosocial acquaintances in daily life. |
| **27** | Actively affiliated with criminal gang / street crew? | Mark **Yes** if documented or self-admitted member of a gang, neighborhood crew, or illicit enterprise. |

### 1.6 Alcohol / Drug Problem
*Evaluates lifetime substance use history, current disruption, school/work impairment, and legal violations.*

| Item # | Assessment Item | Scoring Rule & Guidance |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **28** | Alcohol problem, ever? | Mark **Yes** if participant has ever experienced significant life, health, or legal consequences from alcohol use. |
| **29** | Drug problem, ever? | Mark **Yes** if participant has ever experienced dependence, abuse, or legal consequences from illicit drug use. |
| **30** | Alcohol problem, currently (0–3) | **3:** Complete abstinence or healthy non-problematic use. **2:** Minor situational concern. **1:** Regular excessive drinking. **0:** Active addiction, severe daily impairment. |
| **31** | Drug problem, currently (0–3) | **3:** Clean & sober, active recovery. **2:** Remission, mild cravings. **1:** Regular illicit drug use. **0:** Severe active dependence, daily drug-seeking. |
| **32** | Law violations related to substance abuse? | Mark **Yes** if past offenses occurred while under influence or directly to obtain drugs/alcohol (DUI, drug possession/distribution). |
| **33** | Marital / Family problems related to substance abuse? | Mark **Yes** if substance use caused divorce, separations, estrangement, or domestic conflict. |
| **34** | School / Work problems related to substance abuse? | Mark **Yes** if missed work, lost jobs, failed drug screens, or dropped out of school due to substances. |
| **35** | Physical health / Medical indicators of substance abuse? | Mark **Yes** if history of overdose, liver damage, withdrawal, medical detox, or inpatient rehabilitation. |

## 4. Section 2: Specific Risk / Need Factors
Assessors evaluate non-criminogenic barriers and specific responsivity factors that influence case management:
- **2.1 Personal Problems with Criminogenic Potential:** Racism/sexism, peer pressure vulnerability, gambling, severe emotional volatility.
- **2.2 History of Perpetration:** Documented assault, weapon violence, domestic abuse, or flight risk.
- **2.3 Supportive Needs:** Housing instability, lack of transportation, child care, literacy, medical/dental needs.
- **2.4 Special Responsivity:** Cognitive-behavioral readiness, trauma history, learning style, and literacy accommodations.

## 5. IF / THEN Validation Rules Sheet
To ensure mathematical and logical integrity between interview responses and official scoring forms, apply these verified consistency rules:

| # | Condition (IF) | Mandatory Logic Requirement (THEN) |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **R-1** | Item #2 scores **Yes** (2+ prior convictions) | Item #1 **MUST** score **Yes** (any prior conviction). |
| **R-2** | Item #3 scores **Yes** (3+ prior convictions) | Item #1 and Item #2 **MUST BOTH** score **Yes**. |
| **R-3** | Item #30 scores **0** or **1** (Current Alcohol Problem) | Item #28 (Alcohol Problem Ever) **MUST** score **Yes**. |
| **R-4** | Item #31 scores **0** or **1** (Current Drug Problem) | Item #29 (Drug Problem Ever) **MUST** score **Yes**. |
| **R-5** | Item #30 or #31 scores **0** or **1** (Active substance problem) | At least ONE of #32 (Law), #33 (Family), #34 (Work), or #35 (Health) **MUST** score **Yes**. |
| **R-6** | Gang Participation (Section 2, Item 18) scores **Yes** | Item #24 (Criminal Companions) **MUST** score **Yes**, and Item #25 **MUST** score **0** or **1**. |
| **R-7** | Item #11 scores **Yes** (Never employed full year) | Item #10 (Frequently unemployed) should strongly be considered for **Yes**. |
| **R-8** | Criminal History score is **0** | Items #1, #2, #3, #4, #5, #6, #7, #8 **MUST ALL** be **No / 0**. |
| **R-9** | Area scored as a **Strength** | No item in that subcomponent may be scored **0** or **1**, or marked as an elevated risk indicator. |

## 6. Criminogenic Thinking Patterns & Cognitive Distortions
Turn90 cognitive-behavioral curriculum focuses on recognizing and restructuring these core thinking errors:

| Thinking Error | Description & Criminogenic Mechanism | Typical Participant Verbalizations | Prosocial Turn90 Replacement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mollification / Rationalization** | Making excuses, minimizing harm, blaming the victim or society. | *"What I did isn't as bad as what other people do."* / *"I wasn't hurting anybody."* | Direct personal accountability; recognizing that rules protect everyone. |
| **Cut-Off** | Giving up at the first obstacle; impulsive disregard for consequences. | *"Screw it, whatever happens, happens."* / *"I don't care anymore."* | Pause and breathe (STAC method); evaluating long-term freedom over short-term relief. |
| **Sentimentality** | Believing one good deed offsets chronic rule-breaking. | *"I do a lot for my community, so my charges don't define me."* | Consistency in daily behavior; character is shown by everyday reliability. |
| **Super-Optimism** | Believing one can engage in crime without ever getting caught. | *"I'm too smart to get caught this time."* / *"Lightning won't strike twice."* | Reality testing; recognizing that criminal behavior always ends in prison or death. |
| **Cognitive Indolence** | Lazy thinking; taking the easiest path; avoiding sustained mental effort. | *"I'll figure it out later."* / *"That's too much paperwork."* | Discipline and persistence; breaking large tasks into manageable action steps. |
| **Power Orientation** | Equating aggression and intimidation with respect and safety. | *"In my neighborhood, if you don't strike first, you're food."* | Real strength is de-escalation, emotional control, and providing for family. |
